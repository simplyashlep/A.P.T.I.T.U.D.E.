\section{Nationwide Consumer Reporting Agency Profile}

This section incorporates the detailed Beacon Scoring Engine baseline for the
Nationwide Consumer Reporting Agency (NCRA) class. It is a vertical profile,
not a special scoring engine for Equifax.

\subsection{Class Definition}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{4cm}p{10cm}}
\toprule
Institution class & Nationwide Consumer Reporting Agency (NCRA) \\
Regulatory framework & FCRA, 15 U.S.C. \\S 1681 et seq.; Regulation V, 12 C.F.R. Part 1022 \\
Primary regulator & Consumer Financial Protection Bureau \\
Secondary regulators & FTC, state regulators, and state attorneys general \\
Primary peer class & Three nationwide CRAs; specialty CRAs are a secondary cohort \\

Each metric retains the Beacon specification's definition, weight, source
requirements, direction, normalization bounds, minimum sample rules, flags,
and hard-floor or cap behavior. These must be represented in the executable
metric registry before an empirical score is published.

\subsection{Complete Evidence Inventory}

The NCRA intake should be capable of cataloging, without treating every item
as score-eligible:

\begin{itemize}
  \item CFPB enforcement actions, consent orders, complaint data, and public
        research;
  \item FTC actions, state attorney general actions, and court decisions;
  \item SEC filings, annual reports, quarterly filings, legal proceedings,
        risk disclosures, and cybersecurity disclosures;
  \item FCRA litigation, class settlements, appellate decisions, and monitor
        reports;
  \item dispute timelines, reinvestigation outcomes, furnisher notifications,
        security freezes, and consumer disclosure records;
  \item demographic, geographic, language-access, thin-file, and no-file
        research; and
  \item remediation, audit, consent-order monitoring, and recidivism records.
\end{itemize}

Complaint records are observations about reported consumer experiences. They
are not, by themselves, adjudicated findings of wrongdoing and cannot be
converted directly into a negative Alignment Score component.

\subsection{Equifax Pilot Scope}

The first live instance is Equifax as a corporate group. The profile must
separately resolve Equifax Inc., named subsidiaries, business segments,
activities, jurisdictions, legal regimes, and effective periods. Debt
collection or recovery-management activity is recorded only where a source
supports that scoped classification. The pilot must preserve uncertainty
rather than assigning the label to every Equifax entity.

The first output is an auditable evidence and readiness profile. A final
Alignment Score is emitted only when the NCRA profile's dimensional evidence
requirements are satisfied.
