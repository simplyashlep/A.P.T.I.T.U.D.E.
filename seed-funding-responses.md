# NSF America's Seed Fund Project Pitch Responses

## 1. The Technology Innovation

Aptitude is developing a vertical-neutral institutional intelligence system that measures how closely an organization’s observable behavior aligns with its legally defined, publicly stated, and operational potential. The innovation is not a dashboard, search engine, or conventional risk score. It is a provenance-preserving measurement architecture that converts heterogeneous public records into auditable, role-aware, metric-level evidence and computes a reproducible Alignment Score across Fidelity, Consistency, Transparency, Efficiency, Equity, and Accountability.

The high-risk research problem is whether institutional behavior can be measured across fundamentally different domains without collapsing legal meaning, source reliability, organizational structure, or uncertainty into a misleading number. Existing solutions generally address entity search, compliance reporting, governance ratings, or predictive risk scoring separately. They often treat a corporation, agency, facility, subsidiary, or official as an undifferentiated entity; mix allegations with adjudicated findings; rely on opaque model outputs; or aggregate incompatible data without exposing how conclusions were produced. Aptitude treats each observation as a typed, time-bounded claim linked to a source record, citation, jurisdiction, activity, legal regime, confidence level, and versioned vertical profile.

The proposed innovation is a reusable measurement compiler for institutions. A vertical profile defines an entity’s obligations and potential; a source adapter preserves the raw record; entity resolution identifies the precise parent, subsidiary, facility, office, or program; a metric observation maps the record to a defined measure; normalization applies explicit peer-class bounds; and a deterministic scorer produces a result only when evidence and sample requirements are satisfied. Hard floors, inverse measures, source credibility, uncertainty, and insufficient-data states are computationally explicit rather than hidden analyst judgment.

The risk is substantial. Public data is fragmented, inconsistently structured, legally contextual, temporally changing, and incomplete. The system must distinguish a consumer complaint from a regulatory finding, a business activity from a legal classification, and an organization-wide claim from a jurisdiction- or subsidiary-specific fact. It must prevent demographic variables from contaminating case-similarity models while enabling valid disparity analysis. These are unresolved engineering and applied-research problems.

If successful, Aptitude creates a category between search, compliance software, and institutional benchmarking: explainable, cross-vertical measurement of institutional alignment. Adoption is enabled by an evidence trail rather than trust in an unexplained score. Customers can inspect sources, definitions, confidence, missing evidence, and reproducibility references. Equifax and a Department of Corrections provide deliberately different pilots, testing whether one technical core can support distinct institutional verticals.

## 2. The Technical Objectives and Challenges

Phase I will determine whether Aptitude’s evidence-to-measurement architecture can produce valid, reproducible, cross-vertical assessments from real public data. The goal is not to publish an unsupported score; it is to prove the technical chain from source acquisition to auditable metric output and identify conditions requiring an insufficient-data result.

Objective 1—source capture: implement adapters for the CFPB Consumer Complaint Database and selected SEC, FTC, CourtListener, and public-agency sources. Each adapter will preserve raw responses, retrieval time, request parameters, source version, checksum, licensing metadata, and parser version. Changing schemas, pagination, rate limits, duplicates, inaccessible documents, and non-machine-readable material will be managed with immutable snapshots, idempotent ingestion, retry/backoff, adapter contracts, fixtures, and explicit failure states.

Objective 2—entity and role resolution: construct a versioned entity graph containing aliases, identifiers, ownership, role assertions, effective dates, and source-backed confidence. Equifax will test separation of parent, subsidiaries, business lines, jurisdictions, and activities, including jurisdiction-specific debt-collection or recovery-management activity. The challenge is preventing a fact about one subsidiary or activity from being inherited by the corporate group. We will require source-to-role assertions, preserve unresolved alternatives, and route low-confidence matches for review.

Objective 3—metric construction: implement the Beacon-derived registry and observation layer for the Nationwide Consumer Reporting Agency and Corrections profiles. Observations will contain metric code, raw value, unit, direction, normalization bounds, sample size, provenance, confidence, and flags. The challenge is semantic validity: a complaint is not proof of wrongdoing, a disclosure rate is not a legal conclusion, and disparity requires a defensible denominator. Metric-specific transformations, source eligibility, minimum samples, claim types, and “not measurable” outputs will manage this risk.

Objective 4—reproducible scoring: aggregate sufficient observations, apply credibility and profile weights, enforce floors and caps, and emit Alignment Score, Alignment Status, dimensions, missing requirements, and audit hashes. Identical snapshots will be rerun through independent execution paths and compared byte-for-byte. Equifax and Corrections fixtures will prove that profiles change definitions and evidence requirements without changing the universal contract.

Objective 5—validity boundaries: test duplicates, conflicting findings, stale records, ambiguous matches, suppressed demographic cells, self-reported claims, and insufficient peer classes. The system will fail closed, record reasons, expose review queues, and produce a readiness report. Phase I success is a validated reusable pipeline, not merely a numerical result.

## 3. The Market Opportunity

Aptitude addresses the gap between public-record search, compliance management, ESG-style reporting, and opaque institutional risk models. Customers include oversight bodies, public-interest organizations, investigative and legal researchers, responsible procurement teams, insurers and lenders conducting institutional due diligence, and organizations seeking defensible internal measurement. Individuals and communities benefit from clearer explanations of institutional promises, conduct, and what the public record establishes.

Initial markets are data-rich, high-consequence environments: consumer reporting and financial-data companies, government agencies, corrections, courts, health authorities, regulated providers, and contractors. Uses include evidence research, monitoring, benchmarking, audit preparation, procurement review, and public profiles.

Competitors provide records, alerts, compliance workflows, governance ratings, or domain-specific analytics. Aptitude connects these into a transparent chain: role-aware entity resolution, provenance, vertical profiles, explicit uncertainty, cross-domain metrics, and reproducible scoring. The advantage is inspectable conclusions without treating unlike evidence as equivalent.

The wedge is paid research and monitoring for organizations already spending on fragmented diligence. Equifax is a compelling first case because records span regulation, litigation, complaints, filings, cybersecurity, and disputes. A Corrections profile demonstrates that the platform is not credit-specific. Proven cross-vertical measurement enables expansion through profiles and API access.

## 4. The Company and Team

Aptitude Systems is developing a civic-intelligence and institutional-measurement platform based on the Beacon Scoring Engine specification and its successor, Alignment.V1. The team has already defined the difficult boundaries: six-dimensional measurement, metric weights, evidence sufficiency, source credibility, hard floors, provenance, reproducibility, and separation between universal contracts and vertical profiles. The prototype includes deterministic score contracts, evidence normalization, NCRA and Corrections registries, a metric-observation layer, and a public-record research interface.

Phase I is deliberately research-oriented. We will validate the architecture against Equifax and a Department of Corrections rather than optimize prematurely for one customer or dataset. This creates a meaningful cross-domain test of entity resolution, legal-role classification, source capture, metric construction, statistical sufficiency, and explainable output.

The principal gaps are production-scale ingestion, data engineering, statistical validation, legal-domain review, security, and independent evaluation. We will address them with targeted contractors and advisors in public-data engineering, database and information retrieval, applied statistics and fairness evaluation, consumer-finance and administrative law, and privacy/security. External reviewers will challenge metric definitions and source interpretations before publication. Change control, reproducibility audits, and independent pilot assessment will be required.

The team will keep claims proportional to evidence. Phase I success is a validated reusable measurement pipeline and defensible readiness determination—not an unsupported universal ranking.
